# Marcolou Review

Review the product as a coherent commercial experience, not as a collection of attractive screens.

Read [references/viral-product-principles.md](references/viral-product-principles.md) before the first review in a task. Apply only the principles relevant to the surface under review.

## Workflow

1. Establish the product purpose, primary audience, desired user outcome, business model, route role, and evidence available.
2. Classify the surface as acquisition, evaluation, activation, core workflow, upgrade, retention, or sharing.
3. State the single idea the surface must communicate and the single next action it should privilege.
4. Review hierarchy, copy, proof, product demonstration, pricing visibility, choice count, memorability, and shareability against the reference.
5. Identify contradictions between principles and resolve them from product evidence. Never force a hard paywall, one-time payment, removal of a free plan, or premium pricing without validating economics, trust, procurement, and user risk.
6. Preserve accessibility, safety, truthfulness, legal constraints, and established user expectations. Do not invent testimonials, numbers, comparisons, scarcity, or proof.
7. During creation, run a checkpoint after information architecture and copy, another after visual hierarchy, and a final checkpoint before implementation handoff.
8. If animation is in scope, coordinate with the applicable motion skill; this skill judges what must attract attention and what action matters, not animation mechanics.

## Required output

Start with a one-sentence verdict. Then use one table:

| Surface | Principle | Evidence now | Recommendation | Priority | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use priorities `P0` (blocks comprehension or trust), `P1` (material conversion or activation risk), `P2` (meaningful polish), and `P3` (experiment).

After the table, include:

- **Keep:** what already supports the product's purpose.
- **Resolve:** principle conflicts or missing evidence that require a decision.
- **Validate:** measurable hypotheses and the smallest test for each high-impact change.

For a multi-route product, finish with a route matrix that assigns each route one primary message, one primary action, and no more than three relevant principles.

## Review posture

- Prefer concrete numbers to unsupported adjectives, but request evidence when numbers are unavailable.
- Prefer one dominant action, not necessarily one literal button; secondary actions may remain when required for navigation, accessibility, or recovery.
- Show the product before explaining it when a safe, representative demonstration exists.
- Treat dashboards differently from landing pages: daily operational screens optimize speed, legibility, trust, and task completion rather than virality.
- Treat public, authenticated, internal-admin, and safety-critical routes as distinct contexts.
- Reject generic copy that a competitor could reuse unchanged.
- Recommend experiments for uncertain commercial claims; do not present patterns as universal facts.
